Article, Hepatology Research, 2026

Autoimmune Hepatitis Profiles After Liver Transplantation in Latin American Patients: A Cluster Analysis of Clinical Outcomes

Passos PRC, Bastos de Medeiros I, David TF, Santos Freire MM, Amorim LP, et al.

Summary

Most evidence on liver transplantation for autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) comes from European cohorts. This study analyzed 164 patients from Brazil and Venezuela transplanted between 2002 and 2025, the largest Latin American AIH transplant cohort to date. Random survival forests identified predictors of overall survival, graft survival and AIH recurrence, and clustering on predicted risk separated high-risk groups with clearly worse outcomes.

Key findings

  • Overall survival was 83%, 78% and 49% at 5, 10 and 20 years; AIH recurrence rose from 6% at 5 years to 34% at 20 years.
  • Mycophenolate sodium in maintenance therapy was the top predictor of overall survival (HR 0.44) and graft survival (HR 0.46); younger age was the top risk factor for recurrence.
  • Penalized models predicted 1-year outcomes well (AUC 0.73 for survival and graft survival, 0.81 for recurrence).
  • In a 6-month landmark analysis, infectious complications were the main determinant of long-term survival, and a new model reached AUCs of 0.76 and 0.79 at 5 and 10 years.
